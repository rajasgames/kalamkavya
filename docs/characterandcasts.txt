Character & Cast
This section allows you to build a wide variety of characters and casts, which can be modified later and integrated into the master flowchart. For example, a user can create a character named Vayu and select "deity" as their entity type. Based on project needs, the user can then navigate to the master flowchart, right-click on deities to create a new entity, define the nature of that deity (such as the deity of winds), and assign the character to it during character creation.

1 — 33 Koti Devas (Vayu, Agni, Surya, Vasus, Rudras, Adityas as individuals, Ashwins, etc.)
Lightest template — these are functional forces first, personalities second.
Lineage & Heritage
Cosmic Origin (e.g., born of Brahma's breath/sacrifice, or a Vedic hymn)
Parent Principle (symbolic, not literal — e.g., "child of Akasha")
Deva-gana (which of the 33 classes: 12 Adityas / 11 Rudras / 8 Vasus / 2 Ashwins)
Tattva-lineage (which Mahabhuta or subtle principle they embody)
Hierarchy
Individual (the deity)
Cosmic Function (what natural phenomenon they regulate)
Divine Jurisdiction (scope/limits of power)
Modes of Invocation (associated mantra, yajna, offering)
States of Manifestation (gentle vs. fierce form)
Elemental Attributes (movement, heat, purification, etc.)
Goals & Fears
Cosmic Duty (primary function)
Domain Tension (paradox inherent to the force, e.g., wind is invisible yet vital)
Imbalance Threat (what happens if this force goes unchecked or is suppressed)
Personality
Symbolic Temperament (restless, fierce, nurturing, etc.)
Traits (elemental, not psychological)
Domain Excess (destructive form)
Domain Benevolence (sustaining form)
Physical
Iconographic Form (arms, color, vahana)
Elemental Form (subtle vs. anthropomorphic)
Symbols/Weapons
Voice
Scriptural epithet or invocation line
Associated Boons (what devotees receive)

2 — Regents / Guardians (Ashta-Dikpalas, individual Adityas like Mitra-Varuna, Yama, Kubera, etc.)
Adds jurisdiction, rivalries, and court/relationship structure — these deities administer cosmic order, not just embody a force.
Everything in Tier 1, plus:
Lineage & Heritage
Court/Affiliation (e.g., Varuna as king of the Adityas, guardian of the West)
Consort(s) and Family (often present at this tier — e.g., Kubera's family)
Relationship to Tridev (which supreme deity they serve/report to functionally)
Hierarchy
Administrative Role (Dikpala direction, cosmic office held)
Subordinates (attendants, ganas, or lesser devas under them)
Rivalries/Alliances (e.g., Indra vs. Vritra dynamics)
Goals & Fears
Duty to Cosmic Order (Rita/Dharma maintenance)
Political Fear (loss of realm/throne — Indra's perennial insecurity is the classic case)
External Threat (asuras, rival devas, encroaching entropy)
Character Arc
Historical Shifts (how their prominence rose/fell across Vedic → Puranic periods — e.g., Indra's Vedic centrality vs. later diminishment)
Physical/Voice — same as Tier 1, with added court iconography (throne, capital city like Amaravati, retinue)

3 — Tridev / Tridevi (Brahma-Vishnu-Shiva, Saraswati-Lakshmi-Parvati)
Full theological template — these carry cosmogonic function, avatars, philosophy, and multiple textual traditions.
Everything in Tier 2, plus:
Lineage & Heritage
Position in Trimurti/Tridevi (Creation/Preservation/Destruction, or corresponding shakti function)
Consort Pairing (Shiva-Parvati, Vishnu-Lakshmi, Brahma-Saraswati)
Avatars/Forms (Vishnu's Dashavatara, Shiva's Rudra forms, Devi's Mahavidyas, etc.)
Sampradaya Affiliation (which tradition — Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism — centers this deity as supreme)
Hierarchy
Cosmogonic Function (creation/preservation/dissolution, or corresponding shakti role)
Relationship to Para-Brahman (how this deity is understood as an aspect/expression of the Absolute)
Governance over Devas (how they command or transcend the 33 koti and regents)
Goals & Fears
Cosmic Mandate (maintaining a specific phase of the cosmic cycle)
Philosophical Tension (e.g., Shiva as both ascetic and householder; Vishnu's sleep vs. active intervention)
Threat to Dharma (adharmic forces requiring avatar intervention)
Character Arc
Across Yugas (how the deity's role/intervention style shifts through the four yugas)
Across Avatars (arc across major incarnation stories, if applicable)
Personality
Core Motivations (dharma-preservation, world-maintenance, etc.)
Philosophical Virtues/Flaws (framed as doctrinal tension, not moral failing — e.g., not "flaw" but "productive paradox")
Physical
Primary Iconographic Form + major variant forms
Associated weapons/symbols/vahana
Temple/Purana textual variance in depiction
Voice
Canonical scriptural quotes (Bhagavad Gita for Vishnu/Krishna, Shiva Sutras, Devi Mahatmya, etc.)
Philosophical vocabulary associated with their tradition

4 — Para-Brahman / Absolute
Minimal template — deliberately sparse, since this tier is formless, attributeless (nirguna), and resists individual-character framing entirely.
Nature
Nirguna vs. Saguna description (attributeless Absolute vs. its manifest form, e.g., Ishwara)
Relationship to Tier 3 (how Trimurti/Tridevi emerge from or express this principle — Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Shakta traditions each answer differently)
Textual Basis (Upanishadic description — Brahman in Chandogya, Mandukya, etc.)
Hierarchy
Position: source/ground of the entire flowchart — everything above (Individual → Tridev) is a manifestation or emanation
No jurisdiction, goals, fears, physical form, or arc — these categories don't apply at this tier by definition
Voice
Canonical Mahavakya (great sayings) if you want a "quote" field — e.g., from Upanishads
